Endometriosis Treatment at Punya Hospital

Endometriosis is a condition where tissue similar to the lining of the uterus grows outside the uterus. These growths may occur on the ovaries, fallopian tubes, pelvic lining, bladder, bowel, and surrounding pelvic structures. The condition can cause severe menstrual pain, chronic pelvic discomfort, heavy periods, and fertility problems.

During the menstrual cycle, endometrial-like tissue outside the uterus responds to hormonal changes just like the uterine lining. This can result in inflammation, bleeding, scar tissue formation, and adhesions, leading to pain and reproductive health complications over time.

Symptoms of Endometriosis

The symptoms of endometriosis vary from person to person. Some women experience severe symptoms, while others may have mild symptoms or remain symptom-free for years.

Common Symptoms :

  • Pelvic Pain
  • Painful Periods
  • Heavy Menstrual Bleeding
  • Pain During Intercourse
  • Fertility Issues

Causes Factors

While the exact cause of endometriosis is not fully understood, several factors are believed to contribute to its development.

  • Hormonal imbalances that stimulate the growth of endometrial-like tissue
  • Genetic factors that may increase the risk in women with a family history of endometriosis
  • Immune system dysfunction that may prevent the body from removing abnormal tissue growth
  • Retrograde menstruation, where menstrual blood flows backwards into the pelvic cavity
  • Cellular changes that may cause normal cells outside the uterus to develop into endometrial-like tissue

Risk Factors

Certain factors may increase a woman's likelihood of developing endometriosis. Understanding these risk factors can help with early detection, timely diagnosis, and appropriate management of the condition.

  • Heavy or prolonged menstrual cycles (lasting more than 7 days)
  • Early onset of menstruation
  • Short menstrual cycles (less than 21 days apart)
  • Hormonal imbalances
  • Never having been pregnant
Fertility & Endometriosis

How Endometriosis Affects Fertility ?

Endometriosis is one of the leading causes of infertility among women of reproductive age. Some of the ways endometriosis may affect fertility include

Impact on Ovaries

Endometriosis can affect the ovaries by causing inflammation and the formation of ovarian cysts known as endometriomas. These changes may impact ovarian function and reduce the quality and quantity of healthy eggs. In some cases, this can make conception more challenging.

Fallopian Tube Blockage

Endometriosis-related inflammation and scar tissue can lead to partial or complete blockage of the fallopian tubes. This may interfere with the movement of the egg and sperm, reducing the chances of fertilisation. Early diagnosis and appropriate treatment can help address these concerns.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis of Endometriosis

To accurately identify endometriosis and assess the extent of the condition, your doctor may recommend the following diagnostic evaluations

Medical History and Pelvic Examination

A detailed review of symptoms, menstrual history, and reproductive health helps identify signs of endometriosis. Symptoms such as severe menstrual cramps, chronic pelvic pain, painful intercourse, painful bowel movements, or difficulty conceiving may indicate the condition. During a pelvic examination, the doctor may detect tenderness, nodules, scarring, or restricted movement of pelvic organs caused by endometrial implants and adhesions.

Ultrasound

Ultrasound is commonly used to evaluate the pelvic organs and detect ovarian endometriosis, also known as endometriomas or "chocolate cysts." It can identify cysts formed due to endometrial tissue within the ovaries and may reveal indirect signs of endometriosis. However, smaller endometrial lesions are often not visible on ultrasound imaging.

MRI Scan

MRI provides detailed images of the pelvic organs and surrounding tissues, helping assess the extent and severity of endometriosis. It is particularly useful for identifying deep infiltrating endometriosis and determining whether the condition involves structures such as the bowel, bladder, uterosacral ligaments, or other pelvic tissues.

Diagnostic Laparoscopy

Diagnostic laparoscopy is considered the gold standard for confirming endometriosis. This minimally invasive procedure allows the surgeon to directly visualize endometrial implants, scar tissue, and adhesions inside the pelvic cavity. It helps accurately determine the location, extent, and severity of the disease and may also allow treatment to be performed during the same procedure.

Medical History and Pelvic Examination

A thorough medical history and pelvic examination are essential for diagnosing endometriosis. Your doctor will ask about your symptoms, menstrual cycle, and family history. During the pelvic exam, the doctor will check for any abnormalities in the reproductive organs.

Ultrasound

An ultrasound uses sound waves to create images of the pelvic organs, helping to identify endometrial implants and cysts associated with endometriosis. This non-invasive procedure is often used to confirm the diagnosis and assess the extent of the disease.

MRI Scan

An MRI scan provides detailed images of the pelvic area, allowing doctors to visualize endometrial implants and assess the extent of the disease. This imaging technique is particularly useful when other diagnostic methods are inconclusive or when a more comprehensive evaluation is needed.

Surgical Expertise

Advanced Surgical Procedures for Endometriosis

For moderate to severe endometriosis, surgery may be recommended to remove endometriotic tissue and improve symptoms. Depending on the extent of the condition and individual treatment needs, the following surgical procedures may be considered

Minimally Invasive

Laparoscopic Endometrial Cystectomy

This minimally invasive procedure involves the surgical removal of ovarian endometriomas (chocolate cysts) caused by endometriosis while preserving as much healthy ovarian tissue as possible. The procedure helps relieve pelvic pain, reduce the risk of cyst recurrence, and may improve fertility outcomes in selected patients. The laparoscopic approach offers smaller incisions, reduced postoperative discomfort, and a faster recovery compared to open surgery.

Minimally Invasive

Laparoscopic Oophorectomy

Laparoscopic Oophorectomy is a minimally invasive surgery to remove one (unilateral) or both (bilateral) ovaries. It may be recommended when an ovary is severely affected by disease or when other treatments are not suitable.

Common indications include:

  • Large or persistent ovarian cysts
  • Severe ovarian endometriosis
  • Suspicious ovarian masses or tumors
  • Chronic pelvic pain related to ovarian disease
  • Ovarian torsion requiring urgent treatment
  • High risk of ovarian cancer in selected patients
Advanced Procedure

Laparoscopic Hysterectomy with Bilateral Salpingo-Oophorectomy

A laparoscopic hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy may be recommended in selected cases when symptoms are severe and other treatment options have not provided adequate relief. This minimally invasive procedure involves the removal of the uterus, both fallopian tubes, and both ovaries.

Common indications include:

  • Severe endometriosis with persistent pelvic pain
  • Recurrent ovarian endometriomas (chocolate cysts)
  • Heavy menstrual bleeding affecting quality of life
  • Coexisting uterine conditions such as fibroids or adenomyosis
  • Extensive pelvic disease not responding to conservative treatment

Recovery and Follow-Up Care

Long-term management is essential for maintaining symptom relief and preventing recurrence. Key aspects of recovery and follow-up care include

Post-Treatment Recovery

Recovery plans are tailored according to the type of treatment received and the individual patient's needs. Patients are provided with guidance on activity levels, symptom monitoring, and follow-up care. The goal is to support a smooth recovery and a safe return to daily activities.

Follow-Up Consultations

Regular follow-up appointments help assess recovery, monitor symptoms, and evaluate treatment outcomes. These consultations allow the doctor to address any concerns and make adjustments to the treatment plan if needed. Ongoing reviews play an important role in long-term symptom management.

Long-Term Symptom Management

Endometriosis is often a long-term condition that may require continued care even after treatment. Management strategies may include medications, hormonal therapy, fertility planning, and lifestyle modifications. The aim is to minimise symptoms, reduce recurrence, and improve quality of life.

Lifestyle Recommendations

Healthy lifestyle habits can help support overall well-being and complement medical treatment. A balanced diet, regular physical activity, stress management, and adequate sleep may contribute to symptom control and improved health. Routine medical reviews are also important for monitoring long-term progress.
